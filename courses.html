ICS0020 Logging and Monitoring

Course code: ICS0020
English title: Logging and Monitoring
ECTS: 6
Semester: Autumn 2026/2027
Language: English
Study format: Daytime; 2 academic hours of lectures and 2 academic hours of practical work per week
Target group: Compulsory for IAAB17/26 and IVSB17/26
Assessment: Examination; numeric grade 0–5
Moodle: ICS0020 course materials

Course description

An introduction to operating system and application logging and monitoring, combining concepts and standards with practical work in Linux and Windows environments. Topics include syslog and rsyslog, log aggregation, relay and archiving, regular expressions, logging in applications and scripts, correlation and analysis with Elastic/OpenSearch, and monitoring with Prometheus/Grafana. The course also covers SOC and operational processes, ITIL, log integrity, interoperability, and the architecture of centralized monitoring and log management.

Learning outcomes

  • Choose an appropriate approach to centralized monitoring, log management, and intelligence.
  • Work with operating system logs in Linux and Windows.
  • Define logging requirements and implement logging in applications and scripts.
  • Implement centralized log management and monitoring solutions.
  • Work with rsyslog and perform event correlation.
  • Define the operational processes needed for monitoring and log management.

Prerequisites and tools

Fundamentals of Networking (ICA0013 or ICA0019), basic command-line skills, and basic TCP/IP knowledge. Linux basics are revised early in the course. Students need a computer capable of running two virtual machines simultaneously (minimum 8 GB RAM; 40 GB free disk space recommended), VirtualBox or VMware Workstation Player, Ubuntu Server VMs, and a Windows system for Windows Event Log exercises. Tools include rsyslog, syslog-ng, Fluentd/Fluent Bit, Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, Kibana, Prometheus, Grafana, and Docker.

Schedule and assessment

Wednesdays: 2 September–16 December 2026
Lab: 14:00–15:30, ICO-319 / ICO-317
Lecture: 17:45–19:15, ICO-314

  • Practical assignments: 50%. At least five submissions are needed for the full homework component. Lab attendance is optional, but homework submission is required.
  • Linux From Scratch alternative: Build, document, and present a working LFS system instead of the regular homework track. Register within the first four weeks and present no later than Week 12.
  • Final examination: 50%. 16 December 2026, in Moodle, on site. Covers all course topics, including questions based on log extracts. Passing the examination requires at least 51% of its points.
  • Bonus: Up to 30 additional points for attendance, presentations or extra contributions, and team class activity.

A positive course grade requires at least 51 points in total. Grades are 1 for 51–60, 2 for 61–70, 3 for 71–80, 4 for 81–90, and 5 for 91 points or more; totals above 100 remain grade 5.

ICS0022 Secure Programming

Course code: ICS0022
English title: Secure Programming
ECTS: 6
Semester: Autumn 2026/2027
Language: English
Study format: Daytime; 2 academic hours of lectures and 2 academic hours of practical work per week
Target group: Compulsory for IVSB17/26; elective for IVCM25/26 and VAMM23/26
Assessment: Examination; numeric grade 0–5
Moodle: ICS0022 course materials

Course description

An introduction to secure programming concepts and practical defensive techniques. Topics include input validation, attack-resistant output, race conditions, buffer overflows, injection attacks, protecting secret information, secure memory use and erasing, cryptography, and the challenges of multi-user systems. Lectures and isolated labs use C and Python examples, debugging, and static and dynamic analysis. A semester project applies the techniques to a larger case.

Learning outcomes

  • Understand the basic concepts and common techniques of secure programming.
  • Apply secure programming techniques to practical problems.
  • Understand secure memory usage and secure erasing.
  • Apply secure programming techniques across common programming languages.

Prerequisites and tools

No formal prerequisite courses. Students should be able to read and write code in at least one language, use the Linux command line, and understand basic program memory use. A computer capable of running a Linux VM is required, with VirtualBox or VMware Workstation Player, gcc, GDB, Python 3, and the analysis tools introduced during the course. Exploitation exercises use the supplied practice code in an isolated VM or course-provided systems.

Schedule and assessment

Fridays: 4 September–18 December 2026
Lab: 14:15–15:45, ICO-319 / ICO-317
Lecture: 18:00–19:45, ICO-316

  • Midterm examination: 30%. 23 October 2026, covering Weeks 1–7.
  • Final project: 30%. Three checkpoints worth 5% each on 25 September, 30 October, and 20 November 2026; an in-class presentation worth 15% on 4 or 11 December 2026. The report and code are due before the Week 14 presentation session on 4 December.
  • Version control: Submit a personal Git or equivalent username in Moodle by the end of Week 2 and commit work at each checkpoint.
  • Final examination: 40%. 18 December 2026, including code-analysis questions. Passing the examination requires at least 51% of its points.
  • Bonus: Up to 30 additional points for attendance, submitting at least two thirds of lab homework, and team class activity.

A positive course grade requires at least 51 points in total. Grades are 1 for 51–60, 2 for 61–70, 3 for 71–80, 4 for 81–90, and 5 for 91 points or more; totals above 100 remain grade 5.

ICS0027 Web Application Security

Course code: ICS0027
English title: Web Application Security
ECTS: 6
Semester: Autumn 2026/2027
Language: English
Study format: Daytime; 2 academic hours of lectures and 2 academic hours of practical work per week
Target group: Elective for IVCM25/26 and IVSB17/26
Assessment: Pass/fail
Moodle: ICS0027 course materials

Course description

An introduction to common web application attacks, how they work, and how to build effective defences. Topics include HTTP/HTTPS, browser security policies, HTML and JavaScript injection, XSS, cookies and sessions, authentication and authorization, access-control and business-logic flaws, SQL injection, CSRF, clickjacking, command injection, file handling and uploads, and protecting secrets. The semester also covers responsible disclosure, DNS/CDN security, software analysis, and OWASP API Security. Hands-on labs and a semester project apply these ideas to deliberately vulnerable applications.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain common web application attack methods and the concepts behind them.
  • Explain defences and apply them in practice against common web application attacks.

Prerequisites and tools

No formal prerequisite courses. Students need a basic understanding of web applications and HTTP, basic HTML/JavaScript literacy, and the ability to read simple code; Linux command-line skills are helpful. A computer capable of running a VM or Docker containers is required. Labs use OWASP ZAP or Burp Suite Community Edition, OWASP Juice Shop and other course-provided targets, and PortSwigger Web Security Academy. All course software is free or open source. Testing exercises are limited to the designated practice targets.

Schedule and assessment

Mondays: 31 August–14 December 2026
Lab: 14:00–15:30, ICO-319 / ICO-317
Lecture: 16:00–17:45, ICO-316

  • Midterm: 30%. 19 October 2026, covering Weeks 1–7.
  • Final project: 30%. Three checkpoints worth 5% each on 21 September, 26 October, and 16 November 2026; an in-class presentation worth 15% on 30 November or 7 December 2026. The report and code are due before the Week 14 presentation session on 30 November.
  • Version control: Submit a personal Git or equivalent username in Moodle by the end of Week 2 and commit work at each checkpoint.
  • Final assessment: 40%. 14 December 2026, covering all course topics, including code analysis and attack/defence questions.
  • Bonus: Up to 30 additional points for attendance, PortSwigger Academy lab completion, and team class activity. Register a PortSwigger username in the first or second lab week; the required number of labs is announced in the first lab week.

The course is passed with at least 51 points in total, including bonus points; 50 points or fewer is a fail. No numeric grade is awarded.

Course arrangements

Labs take place before lectures and practise the previous week's topic. There is no lab in Week 1; labs start in Week 2. The semester plans are preliminary, and changes are announced in Moodle. Moodle also contains slides, practical instructions, assignment criteria, deadlines, retake information, the extended syllabuses, and the gradebook.

Examinations and presentations take place on site; tests are taken in Moodle in the classroom. Remote participation requires an agreed exception from the lecturer. AI tools are prohibited during midterms and final tests. For coursework, AI may be used for ideas, explanations, and language editing; its use must be declared and the output verified. Generating submitted code, solutions, or substantive report content requires explicit permission in the assignment.
